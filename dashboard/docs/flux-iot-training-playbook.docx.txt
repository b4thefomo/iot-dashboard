1. Training as a Strategic Asset
Training is not a cost centre or an afterthought. It is a strategic weapon that serves multiple business objectives simultaneously.


Why Training Matters for Flux IoT


1. Deepens the Value Stack
Training transforms our offer from a product sale into a capability-building partnership. Competitors sell sensors; we build compliance-competent organisations.


2. Creates Switching Costs
When a customer's entire team is trained and certified through Flux IoT, switching to a competitor means losing that investment. Their certificates, their training records, their competency framework — all tied to us.


3. Reduces Support Burden
Well-trained customers generate fewer support tickets, use the product more effectively, and achieve better outcomes. Training is cheaper than support.


4. Generates Leads
Free training content attracts prospects who are not ready to buy but are ready to learn. They enter our world through education and naturally progress toward purchase.


5. Reinforces Authority Positioning
When we train people on compliance, we are the experts. Every certificate issued carries our brand. Every trained employee becomes an advocate for proper monitoring.


The Training Philosophy
We do not just monitor temperature. We build organisations that understand why temperature matters, how to respond when things go wrong, and how to prove compliance to anyone who asks.

3. Core Curriculum: Temperature Compliance Fundamentals
This is the flagship training programme included with all Professional and Enterprise subscriptions. It establishes baseline competency across the organisation.


Programme Overview
* Total Duration: 45-60 minutes (self-paced)
* Format: Video lessons with interactive quizzes
* Assessment: 20-question final exam (80% pass threshold)
* Certification: Digital certificate upon completion
* Validity: 12 months (annual refresher required)


Module Breakdown


Module 1: Why Temperature Matters
Duration: 10 minutes
Foundational understanding of temperature's impact on product safety, quality, and compliance. Sets the context for why monitoring matters.
Learning Outcomes:
• Explain the science of temperature-sensitive products
• Identify the consequences of temperature excursions
• Describe the regulatory landscape and why it exists
• Articulate the business case for proper temperature control
	

Module 2: Understanding Temperature Zones
Duration: 10 minutes
Deep dive into critical temperature thresholds, danger zones, and product-specific requirements.
Learning Outcomes:
• Define the temperature danger zone (5°C to 63°C)
• Identify correct storage temperatures for different product types
• Explain the two-hour and four-hour rules
• Recognise product-specific requirements in their sector
	

Module 3: Reading and Responding to Alerts
Duration: 10 minutes
Practical training on using the Flux IoT system to monitor temperatures and respond appropriately to alerts.
Learning Outcomes:
• Navigate the Flux IoT dashboard confidently
• Interpret temperature readings and trends
• Respond correctly to different alert types
• Document actions taken during an incident
	

Module 4: Incident Management
Duration: 10 minutes
Step-by-step protocols for handling temperature excursions, from detection through resolution and documentation.
Learning Outcomes:
• Follow the incident response checklist correctly
• Make appropriate decisions about product disposition
• Escalate issues to the right people at the right time
• Complete incident documentation thoroughly
	

Module 5: Documentation and Audit Readiness
Duration: 10 minutes
Best practices for maintaining records that satisfy regulatory requirements and preparing for inspections.
Learning Outcomes:
• Explain documentation requirements for their sector
• Generate and interpret compliance reports
• Prepare for regulatory inspections confidently
• Locate historical records when needed

4. Sector-Specific Training Tracks
After completing the core curriculum, learners can pursue sector-specific tracks that address the unique regulatory requirements and operational challenges of their industry.


Track A: Food Service and Hospitality
For restaurants, cafes, hotels, catering companies, and commercial kitchens.


* HACCP principles and critical control points
* FSA Food Hygiene Rating requirements
* Walk-in cooler and freezer best practices
* Receiving and storage temperature protocols
* Health inspection preparation
Duration: 30 minutes additional


Track B: Healthcare and Pharmaceutical
For pharmacies, GP surgeries, hospitals, care homes, and vaccine storage facilities.


* MHRA and GDP requirements
* Vaccine storage and handling (Green Book compliance)
* Controlled drug temperature requirements
* CQC inspection readiness
* Cold chain documentation for healthcare
Duration: 30 minutes additional


Track C: Food Manufacturing and Distribution
For food producers, distributors, cold storage facilities, and logistics operators.


* BRC Global Standards requirements
* SALSA certification compliance
* Supply chain temperature documentation
* Customer audit preparation
* Transport and receiving protocols
Duration: 30 minutes additional


Track D: Retail and Supermarkets
For grocery stores, convenience stores, and retail food outlets.


* Display case and cabinet monitoring
* Stock rotation and date management
* Customer complaint handling for temperature issues
* Multi-site compliance coordination
* Trading Standards requirements
Duration: 30 minutes additional

6. Training Delivery Methods


Primary: Self-Paced Online Learning
The foundation of our training programme is a self-paced online platform that learners can access anytime.


Platform Requirements:
* Mobile-responsive design (many learners complete on phones)
* Progress saving (learners can stop and resume)
* Offline capability for areas with poor connectivity
* Multi-language support (future: expand to European markets)
* Accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.1 AA minimum)


Secondary: Live Webinars
Regular live sessions add human connection and allow for Q&A.


Webinar Types:
* Monthly: Open enrollment sessions for new customers
* Quarterly: Regulatory update briefings
* On-demand: Custom sessions for Enterprise customers
* Special: Response to major regulatory changes


Tertiary: On-Site Training (Enterprise)
For Enterprise customers, offer on-site training as a premium add-on.


* Half-day or full-day sessions at customer premises
* Tailored content to their specific operation
* Hands-on system walkthrough with their actual data
* Train-the-trainer options for large organisations


Support Materials
Complement video training with practical reference materials:


* Quick Reference Cards: Laminated cards for kitchens/storage areas
* Incident Response Flowcharts: Visual decision trees
* Temperature Log Templates: Backup paper forms
* Alert Response Checklists: Step-by-step guides
* Regulatory Pocket Guides: Key thresholds and requirements

8. Content Production Guide


Video Production Standards


Format Specifications:
* Resolution: 1080p minimum (4K for future-proofing)
* Aspect ratio: 16:9 for desktop, also create 9:16 clips for mobile
* Length: 3-5 minutes per module segment (attention spans are short)
* Captions: Always include closed captions (accessibility + sound-off viewing)


Production Options:
* Low budget: Screen recording with voiceover (Loom, Camtasia)
* Medium budget: Animated explainer videos (Vyond, Powtoon)
* High budget: Professional presenter with graphics overlay


Start with low-budget production to validate content. Upgrade to higher production quality as you prove demand and refine the curriculum.


Script Writing Framework
Every training module follows this structure:


1. Hook (30 seconds): Why this matters to them personally
2. Context (1 minute): Background and regulatory context
3. Core content (2-3 minutes): The key information they need
4. Application (1 minute): How to apply this in their role
5. Summary (30 seconds): Key takeaways and next steps


Quiz and Assessment Design


Question Types:
* Multiple choice (most common, easy to auto-grade)
* True/false with explanation required
* Scenario-based questions ("What would you do if...")
* Matching exercises for temperature thresholds


Assessment Principles:
* Test application, not just recall ("What should you do?" not "What is the rule?")
* Include realistic scenarios from their industry
* Provide immediate feedback with explanations
* Allow retakes (learning is the goal, not gatekeeping)

9. Training Operations and Administration


Learning Management System (LMS)
Start simple and scale. You do not need enterprise LMS on day one.


Phase 1 (0-50 customers): Basic Setup
* Host videos on Vimeo or Wistia (private, trackable)
* Use Typeform or Google Forms for quizzes
* Generate certificates with Canva or Certifier
* Track completions in a spreadsheet or Airtable


Phase 2 (50-200 customers): Lightweight LMS
* Implement Teachable, Thinkific, or LearnDash
* Automate certificate generation
* Enable customer admin dashboards
* Add progress tracking and reminders


Phase 3 (200+ customers): Enterprise LMS
* Consider TalentLMS, Docebo, or custom build
* SSO integration with customer systems
* API integration with Flux IoT dashboard
* Advanced reporting and analytics


Customer Training Administration
For each customer, track:


* Total staff count and roles
* Completion rates by module
* Certification status and expiry dates
* Refresher training due dates
* Training-related support tickets


Renewal and Refresher Process
1. 60 days before expiry: Automated email reminder to individual
2. 30 days before expiry: Reminder to individual + manager
3. 7 days before expiry: Final reminder with direct link to refresher
4. On expiry: Certificate marked as expired, refresher required
5. Refresher completed: New certificate issued, 12-month validity

10. Measuring Training Effectiveness


Kirkpatrick Model for Training Evaluation
Use the four-level Kirkpatrick Model to measure training impact:


Level
	What It Measures
	How to Measure
	1. Reaction
	Did learners enjoy it?
	Post-training survey (NPS, ratings)
	2. Learning
	Did learners acquire knowledge?
	Quiz scores, assessment results
	3. Behaviour
	Did behaviour change?
	Alert response times, documentation quality
	4. Results
	Did business outcomes improve?
	Inspection results, incident rates, retention
	

Key Training Metrics


Engagement Metrics:
* Completion rate (target: 85%+ of enrolled learners)
* Time to completion (track if modules are too long)
* Drop-off points (identify problematic content)
* Repeat views (indicates confusion or high value)


Learning Metrics:
* Average quiz scores (target: 85%+ pass rate)
* First-attempt pass rate (indicates content clarity)
* Most-missed questions (identify knowledge gaps)


Business Impact Metrics:
* Trained vs untrained customer support tickets
* Correlation between training completion and retention
* Customer inspection pass rates (trained vs untrained)
* Time from onboarding to full productivity


Training as a Retention Indicator
Customers who engage with training are more likely to renew. Track training engagement as a leading indicator of churn risk.


High Engagement (Low Churn Risk)
	Low Engagement (High Churn Risk)
	80%+ staff certified
	<20% staff certified
	Refresher training completed on time
	Expired certificates not renewed
	Attended webinars or advanced training
	No engagement with training content
